abstract: "The microscopic dynamics of metallic glasses are fundamentally governed by their out-of-equilibrium state. In this study, we combine synchrotron x-ray photon correlation spectroscopy with atomistic simulations to track the evolution of structural dynamics from the out-of-equilibrium glassy solid into the meta-equilibrium state. The experiments reveal a clear transition from compressed- to stretched-exponential dynamics driven by physical aging. In contrast, the simulations consistently exhibit stretched-exponential behavior across all tested conditions, including zero pressure and applied hydrostatic stress. Despite these qualitatively different initial responses, we demonstrate here that both experimental and computational approaches converge toward a common meta-equilibrium relaxation shape with β ≈ 0.8. Further, in this regime both experiment and simulation exhibit de Gennes narrowing in the relaxation timescale and shape parameter, a behavior that emerges in the experiments even before the meta-equilibrium state is reached. Ultimately, the absence of compressed-exponential dynamics in simulated quenched-in and pressurized states indicates that the experimental as-cast state possesses a distinct dynamical fingerprint whose exact structural origin continues to remain unresolved."
